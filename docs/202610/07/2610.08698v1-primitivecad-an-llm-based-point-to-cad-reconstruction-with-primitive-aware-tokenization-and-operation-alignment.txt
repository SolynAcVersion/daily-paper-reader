## Abstract 

Large-model-based point-to-CAD generation holds immense po-tential for advancing industrial design and enhancing 3D modeling efficiency. However, most existing methods approach the problem as a general point-cloud encoding and token prediction task, neglect-ing the tokenization and supervision specifically for CAD-related primitives. As a result, these methods often struggle to accurately reconstruct the intricate primitive structures. To address this limi-tation, we propose PrimitiveCAD, a novel multi-stage paradigm for point-to-CAD reconstruction that enhances the geometric accuracy of generated CAD models while better preserving critical geometric features. First, we introduce a primitive-aware point cloud tokeniza-tion model, enabling the system to learn more robust geometric representations from CAD point clouds. Next, we perform super-vised finetuning on a large language model (LLM) and introduce an operation alignment loss to align key CAD operation frequencies, thereby improving the preservation of global shape features. ## 1 Introduction 

Computer-Aided Design (CAD) plays a crucial role in industrial design and manufacturing, enabling the creation and modification arXiv:2610.08698v1 [cs.GR] 6 Oct 2026 MM ’26, November 10–14, 2026, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil Jian Gao, Kailin Bi, Jiamin Xu, Jinlan Xu, & Gang Xu 

of objects across various industries, including architecture, auto-motive, and aerospace. Traditionally, CAD requires designing 2D sketches and performing 3D operations to generate models—a pro-cess that demands specialized knowledge and skills, making it less accessible to non-experts. With the rise of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Large Language Models (LLMs), a new approach called Computer-Automated Design (CAutoD) has emerged, integrating AI to enhance design speed, creativity, and personalization. This evolution simplifies the CAD generation and manipulation process, making it more intuitive and user-friendly for both experts and non-experts. For CAD generation, an effective output model representation is essential. Early methods typically relied on Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) [8] or Boundary Representation (B-Rep) [1]. Re-cent studies, however, have shifted towards encoding CAD models as operation sequences [32] or more flexible representations, such as Python code (e.g., CadQuery [26]), which offer enhanced inter-pretability. The task of converting inputs into Python code aligns well with recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), which excel at in-context learning and multimodal tokenization. At the input level, however, due to the limitations of text descriptions and occlusions in 2D images, LLMs often struggle to generate accu-rate CAD models from simple language or visual descriptions. In contrast, point clouds capture detailed geometric data and can be easily obtained through scanning or direct modeling techniques, making them a superior modality for conditioning and providing more precise guidance for CAD generation. As a result, we con-dition CAD generation on point cloud inputs and generate CAD models as executable Python scripts. The point-to-CAD sequence generation method using LLMs con-verts point cloud data into tokens, which are fed into the LLM as context to generate corresponding CAD sequences using the model’s next-token prediction capability. However, current meth-ods have several key limitations: 1) Insufficient capability to extract CAD features from point clouds; existing methods either train point feature extraction from scratch via end-to-end training or use general point cloud feature extractors like PointNet [23] or Point Transformer [33, 38], which are not specifically designed for CAD features; 2) The supervision for supervised finetuning (SFT) of CAD sequences in LLMs is adapted from general chat models, without adjustments for CAD sequences, leading to suboptimal SFT performance; 3) The reward design in Reinforcement Learn-ing (RL) only considers geometric overlap, neglecting the specific point-line-plane structural features inherent in CAD models. To resolve these issues, our method first trains a primitive-aware tokenizer for point clouds, using Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) [33] as the backbone. We fine-tune PTv3 with geometry primitives de-rived from CAD sketching and extrusion operations, enabling it to produce discriminative features for points sampled from CAD models. Next, we perform SFT to allow the LLM to generate Python code. To enhance SFT supervision, we introduce an operation align-ment loss specifically designed for CAD sequences, which aligns the expected frequency of core CAD operations and imposes macro-level geometric constraints. We found that even with SFT alone, our method performs on par with existing baselines that incorporate RL. Finally, similar to Cadrille [12], we apply RL to further refine the model. In the RL phase, traditional volume-based IoU rewards often fail to capture fine geometric details and provide insufficient guid-ance when small features are missing. To address this, we propose a geometry feature-line alignment reward that explicitly extracts and matches key topological features. Incorporating this RL phase enables our method to generate CAD models with low stochasticity and highly accurate reconstruction performance. In summary, this work systematically integrates CAD structural information into the entire learning pipeline, spanning point-cloud tokenization, SFT, and RL. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We propose a point-to-CAD reconstruction framework with a multi-stage training paradigm. This framework first ex-tracts discriminative features to achieve primitive-aware tokenization for CAD point clouds, and then uses an LLM to generate CAD sequences through SFT and RL. 

• During the SFT phase, we introduce an operation alignment loss that uses a relaxed IoU on the frequency of key opera-tors, establishing macro-level constraints for CAD operation sequences and improving SFT performance. 

• During the RL phase, we introduce a novel feature-line align-ment reward, effectively addressing the limitations of tra-ditional volume-based IoU rewards when handling small geometric features and complex CAD topologies. Experimental results demonstrate that our point-to-CAD recon-struction framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in CAD sequence reconstruction across multiple public benchmarks. 

## 2 Related work 

CAD sequence generation. DeepCAD [32] pioneered sequence-based CAD generation by encoding sketch and extrusion opera-tions as discrete tokens. Although CAD-Diffuser [20] and SECAD-Net [15] further extended this sequence modeling paradigm, they still rely on customized command spaces or task-specific symbolic sequences. CAD-Recode [26] represents CAD models as Python code based on CadQuery [4], enabling the model to directly generate executable parametric modeling programs rather than customized discrete instructions. Recent advances in LLMs have revolutionized CAD sequence generation. LLM-based approaches generate CAD construction se-quences from text [11] or images [37]. CAD-MLLM [34] further supports text, image, and point-cloud inputs using pretrained mul-timodal models. CAD-Recode [26] generates Python programs for point-to-CAD reconstruction, while Cadrille [12] extends this par-adigm to multimodal inputs. Other approaches improve topology selection [24], dimensional precision [25], and CAD program re-finement through visual or geometric feedback [7, 9, 16]. However, these methods rely solely on token-level cross-entropy loss, which does not account for the unique characteristics of CAD operations, limiting the model’s ability to preserve CAD features. Since large-model generation is inherently stochastic, recent approaches like CADCrafter [3], Cadrille [12] and ReCAD [14] use reinforcement learning to reduce stochasticity and improve output quality. However, these methods do not explicitly align fine-grained CAD geometric features. Point-based CAD generation. Early point-to-CAD methods used parametric surface fitting [2], RANSAC [17], or region growing [22] for primitive fitting and object decomposition. Later approaches directly recover geometric structures from point clouds but often rely on predefined primitives or local fitting objectives [19, 30, 39]. ## 3 Proposed Method 3.1 Overview 

Our goal is to generate a CAD model from an input point cloud P,where the target CAD model is represented as an executable Python script [26] that generates the parametric boundary representation (B-Rep). Consequently, the task of generating a CAD model from a point cloud is transformed into a conditional language genera-tion problem. To achieve this, as shown in Fig. 2, we first train a primitive-aware tokenizer for the CAD point cloud (Sec. 3.2), incor-porating point cloud tokens into an LLM [29] and then fine-tune it with ground-truth Python scripts. We also introduce an operation alignment loss to enhance supervision (Sec. 3.3). However, with SFT, the LLM may still have a probabilistic chance of generating undesirable models. To reduce stochasticity, we incorporate RL and use a novel geometry feature alignment reward to further refine the model (Sec. 3.4).

## 3.3 SFT with Operation Alignment

We reserve a placeholder interval of length 256 in the input sequence of the language model and replace the corresponding token embeddings within this interval with point cloud tokens T𝑝𝑐 . This explicit feature alignment strategy allows the model to directly map geometric structure priors and coordinate representations into the reasoning space of the LLM, providing robust cross-modal contextual support for subsequent CAD code generation. ## 3.4 RL with Feature-Line Alignment 

After the SFT stage, we introduce an online reinforcement learning phase where the model is further refined using feedback from pro-grammatic execution to reduce stochasticity and improve geometric accuracy. We use Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) [27] for feedback, which does not require a separate reward model. Instead, it estimates an advantage function based on the relative quality of different samples within a group. At this stage, the paired point cloud and code sequence used for SFT is no longer required. Unlike SFT which relies on annotated CAD reconstruction sequences, RL evaluates the results of the model-generated outputs through execution and feeds the reward signals back into the optimization process. This eliminates the need for complete CAD sequence labels and allows the use of 3D datasets without sequence annotations, significantly expanding the available training data. By learning from more diverse geometric shapes, the LLM becomes more robust to geometric noise and artifacts in real-world scenarios. In each training iteration, given the point cloud tokens, the fine-tuned LLM samples 𝐾 candidate code sequences 𝐶 1, 𝐶 2, . . . , 𝐶 𝐾 in parallel. We execute the generated code to obtain the corresponding CAD models and compute the reward 𝑟 for each sample. The ad-vantage function 𝐴 is calculated based on the normalized rewards within the group. To evaluate the geometric quality of the gener-ated sequences, we design a reward function that considers both global volumetric consistency and local feature fidelity, addressing the limitations of conventional volumetric rewards in capturing fine-grained structures. When the generated code successfully executes into a valid CAD model 𝑀 𝑝𝑟𝑒𝑑 , we align and normalize both the predicted model and the ground-truth model 𝑀 𝑔𝑡 . The global geometric similarity is then measured using the IoU between 𝑀 𝑔𝑡 and 𝑀 𝑝𝑟𝑒𝑑 . Addition-ally, as shown in Fig. 6, local structural similarity 𝑆 𝑓 𝑒𝑎𝑡 , captured through feature-line extraction and matching, is incorporated into MM ’26, November 10–14, 2026, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil Jian Gao, Kailin Bi, Jiamin Xu, Jinlan Xu, & Gang Xu 

the reward function: 

𝑟 = 𝑤 iou · IoU (𝑀 𝑔𝑡 , 𝑀 𝑝𝑟𝑒𝑑 ) + 𝑤 feat · 𝑆 𝑓 𝑒𝑎𝑡 (𝑀 𝑔𝑡 , 𝑀 𝑝𝑟𝑒𝑑 ). (11) We set 𝑤 iou = 0.6 and 𝑤 feat = 0.4. When the generated code fails to execute, we fix the reward 𝑟 to a value of −10. To compute 𝑆 𝑓 𝑒𝑎𝑡 , we perform feature-line matching by calcu-lating the similarity for each feature type (e.g., straight lines, arcs, and circles). This is done by constructing a normalized similarity matrix based on the 1 − 𝐿 2 distance, which incorporates geometric attributes such as endpoint distance, center offset, length ratio, and directional consistency. Since the numbers of feature lines in the ground-truth and predicted models may differ, 𝑆 feat is computed as the optimal normalized matching score using the Hungarian algorithm [13], where unmatched feature lines are assigned zero score in the final aggregation. For feature-line extraction, we extract feature lines in the mesh domain; please refer to the supplementary materials for further information. 

## 4 Experiments 

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, we compare it with representative methods for point-to-CAD reconstruction. The baselines include sketch and extrusion sequence modeling meth-ods, such as DeepCAD [32], Point2Cyl [28], HNC-CAD [36], Multi-CAD [21], TransCAD [5], CAD-Diffuser [20], and CAD-SIGNet [10], as well as recent methods that generate executable programs, in-cluding CAD-Recode [26] and Cadrille [12]. In the ablation study, we examine the effectiveness of the PTv3 [33] finetuning and the operation alignment during the SFT stage. Then we compare the re-construction performance achieved without feature-line alignment reward and with different RL strategies. We also assess the impact of point cloud tokenization under varying input point counts. For implementation details, please refer to the supp. material. 

Datasets. During the SFT stage, to ensure a fair comparison with existing methods for point-to-CAD reconstruction, we adopt the DeepCAD dataset [32], which contains approximately 16k CAD models collected from real design data, as the primary training set for point cloud based supervision. We also incorporate approxi-mately one million models from the CAD-Recode dataset [26] for training, where all CAD models are constructed from executable programs written in CadQuery [4]. During the RL stage, we lever-age the Fusion360 dataset [31], a small scale CAD reconstruction benchmark containing complex and realistic CAD models, to guide the model toward generating CAD structures that better satisfy real world engineering requirements. 

Metrics. Following CAD-Recode [26], we evaluate our method using four metrics: intersection over union (IoU), invalidity rate (IR), mean Chamfer distance (Mean CD), and median Chamfer distance (Med. CD). IoU measures volumetric overlap with the ground truth, IR is the fraction of code failing to compile into valid 3D solids, and CD assesses surface accuracy by sampling 8192 points per model. Both mean and median CD are scaled by 10 3 for easier comparison. For quantitative comparisons, we adopt a single-pass evaluation as in Cadrille [12], without test-time best-of-𝑁 candidate selection. For qualitative comparisons with other LLM-based methods in Fig. 7, we report the best result among 10 generated candidates for each method. For the RL ablation in Fig. 9 and Table 5, we further report both best-of-10 and worst-of-10 results to analyze the effect of RL in reducing stochasticity. 

## 4.1 Evaluation of SFT and RL 

To comprehensively evaluate the proposed method, we conduct quantitative comparisons with representative prior methods on the DeepCAD [32] and Fusion360 [31] test sets, and further compare our method with Cadrille [12] under both the SFT-only and RL-finetuned settings. The quantitative results are reported in Table 1 and Table 2, while qualitative comparisons are shown in Fig. 7. As shown in Table 1, our method achieves the best overall per-formance on both DeepCAD [32] and Fusion360 [31]. In particular, under the same Qwen2VL-2B-Direct [29] backbone as Cadrille [12], our method obtains higher IoU and lower Mean CD and median CD, indicating more accurate CAD reconstruction. As shown in the last two rows of Table 1, the performance of our method can be further enhanced by training on the larger CAD-Recode [26]. These results validate the effectiveness of the primitive-aware tokenization and the proposed SFT design. In Table 2, we evaluate the effectiveness of RL by comparing our method with Cadrille [12] under both the SFT-only and RL-finetuned settings on the Fusion360 dataset [31]. Even without RL, our model already achieves performance on par with the RL-finetuned Cadrille [12]. After RL finetuning, our method consis-tently outperforms Cadrille [12] on all evaluation metrics, demon-strating that the proposed RL stage further improves reconstruc-tion accuracy and validity on top of the SFT baseline. In Fig. 7, we present qualitative CAD reconstruction results from input point clouds. Compared with CAD-Recode [26] and Cadrille [12], our ap-proach can better-preserved fine-grained geometric features, such as holes and right angles. ## 5 Conclusion 

We propose PrimitiveCAD, a multi-stage approach for generating CAD sequences from point clouds. PTv3 fine-tuning with primitive labels improves geometric representations, operation alignment loss enhances global shape accuracy during SFT, and feature-line alignment reward refines geometric details during RL. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in terms of geometric accuracy, code validity, and reconstruction quality. 

Limitation. Currently, our framework mainly focuses on com-ponent level generation and does not yet support complex multi-module assembly models, which will be explored in future work.
