## ABSTRACT 

Reconstructing editable parametric CAD models from a single-view image is of great practical value for modern manufacturing, yet remains challenging due to incomplete geometric observations and complex inter-part relationships. To ad-dress it, we propose CADForge , an agentic framework that progressively converts a single image into CadQuery programs. CADForge decomposes an object into CAD-meaningful components and performs explicit geometric reasoning for each component, a process that first identifies CAD-relevant constraints and then trans-lates them into precise modeling parameters through mathematical code. The in-ferred parameters then drive component-wise synthesis of executable CadQuery programs, with a review agent evaluating the resulting geometry and providing targeted feedback for iterative refinement. To further improve robustness and ef-ficiency, CADForge incorporates a failure-guided toolkit construction mechanism to distill accumulated experience into tools, and maintains a compact parametric CAD memory for retrieving modeling context on demand. Experiments on diverse single- and multi-part objects show that CADForge consistently outperforms ex-isting baselines in reconstruction fidelity and perceptual quality, demonstrating an effective approach to accurate single-view CAD reconstruction. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Computer-aided design (CAD) plays an essential role in modern engineering (Narayan et al. (2013)), where 3D objects can be constructed through sequences of modeling operations and editable pa-rameters. Compared with point clouds and meshes, parametric CAD models can provide precise boundary representations (B-Reps) and flexible editability, making them well suited for real-world applications such as furniture design (Scurtu et al. (2015)), and industrial manufacturing (Campi et al. (2022)). However, creating such models typically requires specialized expertise and consider-able manual effort, driving growing interest in automatic parametric CAD generation. Earlier work explored automatic CAD generation by learning neural representations of CAD con-struction sequences with dedicated network architectures (Wu et al. (2021); Xu et al. (2022; 2023)). With advances in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), current work has made considerable progress in generating parametric CAD models from text descriptions (Khan et al. (2024b); Li et al. (2025a); Guan et al. (2025)), point clouds (Rukhovich et al. (2025); Kabisov et al. (2026); Li et al. (2026b)) or multi-view images (Kolodiazhnyi et al. (2026); Pyatov et al. (2026)), achieving strong performance in both geometric accuracy and structural validity. However, accurate parametric CAD reconstruction from a single-view image, which offers signifi-cant practical value in industrial design and 3D printing (Sachs et al. (1990)), remains challenging. This task is inherently nontrivial as it requires a model not only to infer complete 3D geometry and structural relationships from partial visual observations but also to translate them into precise CAD modeling parameters and commands. Existing methods (Li et al. (2025c); Noeckel et al. (2025); Chen et al. (2025a); Doris et al. (2026a)) primarily rely on task-specific fine-tuning over curated image-CAD datasets and struggle to produce complex multi-part objects like furniture. When ap-plied to in-the-wild images, they often produce distorted geometry, omit important components, or generate disconnected parts. More recently, some studies (Zhou et al. (2026); Lin et al. (2026)) have begun to explore agent-based 3D code generation. Although they can produce visually compelling 1arXiv:2610.04262v1 [cs.AI] 3 Oct 2026 CADForge: Agentic Single-View CAD Reconstruction with Explicit Geometry Reasoning results, they often incur substantial token cost and can’t fully capture the underlying geometric con-straints and inter-component spatial relationships, resulting in unfaithful reconstructions. To address this challenge, we propose CADForge , an agentic framework for single-view CAD re-construction generation that converts an input image into a CadQuery program. Rather than gener-ating the entire object monolithically, CADForge decomposes it into individual parts and constructs the model progressively. For each part, CADForge performs explicit geometry reasoning through two stages: geometry observation and geometry programming . In the first stage, a VLM extracts geometric patterns, proportional cues, and spatial relationships with previously generated parts from the input image. These observations are then translated into mathematical programs that leverage the current CAD state to solve for the key parameters required to construct the part. Based on the obtained specification and parameters, a code generator, assisted by a self-constructed toolkit, generates executable CadQuery code and integrates the new part into the existing model. We also in-troduce a reviewer agent to assess the result for geometric fidelity and topological validity, diagnose potential errors, and provide targeted feedback for iterative refinement. Throughout this process, CADForge introduces a compact parametric CAD memory to record the current modeling state, preserving cross-component consistency with minimal token overhead. We construct an evaluation set covering both part-level and object-level CAD models collected from diverse datasets, and evaluate CADForge against existing methods on it. The results demonstrate that CADForge can more faithfully reconstruct the input image as a parametric CAD model while maintaining competitive token consumption. Our contributions can be summarized as: • We propose CADForge, a multi-stage agentic framework tailored for single-view CAD reconstruction generation that decomposes an object into individual parts and progressively constructs a parametric CAD model through executable CadQuery code. • To accurately capture geometric information from the input image, we introduce an explicit geometry reasoning mechanism that first infers relative geometric relationships among parts and then translates them into key modeling parameters. A compact parametric CAD memory is maintained to ground each reasoning step in the evolving construction state. • We develop a comprehensive benchmark that covers both individual CAD parts and com-plete multi-part objects from diverse data sources. Experimental results demonstrate that CADForge achieves higher reconstruction fidelity than existing methods. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Parametric CAD Generation. Learning-based CAD generation began with efforts to represent construction histories as sequences of sketches and geometric operations (Wu et al. (2021); Xu et al. (2022; 2023)). Building on these representations, advances in foundation models and large language models have inspired a growing body of work on conditional CAD generation. These methods translate textual descriptions into editable CAD sequences or executable programs, with some fur-ther incorporating explicit geometric controls or intermediate model states to support more complex construction procedures (Khan et al. (2024b); Li et al. (2024; 2025a); Zhang et al. (2025); Guan et al. (2025); Qi et al. (2026)). Alongside this development, another line of research models CAD generation as a reverse-engineering problem, seeking to recover the underlying construction process from geometric observations such as point clouds and multi-view images (Khan et al. (2024a); Liu et al. (2024); Ma et al. (2024); Rukhovich et al. (2025); Kabisov et al. (2026)). More recently, sev-eral methods have moved toward unified multimodal frameworks that accommodate heterogeneous inputs, including text, multi-view images, and point clouds, allowing different forms of design in-tent and geometric evidence to be handled within a shared generation backbone (Xu et al. (2024); Li et al. (2025b); Kolodiazhnyi et al. (2026); Pyatov et al. (2026)). Compared with text- or geometry-conditioned settings, single-view CAD reconstruction still faces great challenges. Existing approaches broadly follow two directions: directly predicting CAD com-mand sequences or executable programs (Alam & Ahmed (2024); Chen et al. (2025b;a); Doris et al. (2026a)), and recovering structured B-Reps using learned geometric priors and fitting (Li et al. (2025c)). Most of these methods rely on task-specific training with image-CAD datasets that largely consist of simple CAD models. Consequently, their performance often degrades on out-of-distribution images and relatively complicated objects. In contrast, CADForge adopts a training-free 2CADForge: Agentic Single-View CAD Reconstruction with Explicit Geometry Reasoning agent-based framework, which leverages the visual, geometric, and programming priors of VLMs and generalizes more effectively without specialized fine-tuning. 

Agentic 3D Modeling. Recent advances in large language models and vision-language models have spurred growing interest in agentic 3D modeling, where models plan modeling procedures, interact with 3D software, and refine their outputs through execution and visual feedback. Early systems primarily use LLMs to operate general-purpose 3D software such as Blender, translating natural-language instructions into coordinated modeling actions for object and scene creation (Hu et al. (2024); Sun et al. (2025); Zhou et al. (2025); Lu et al. (2026)). More work has gone beyond tool con-trol by treating executable programs as structured representations of 3D objects themselves. Com-pared with direct mesh representations, code makes semantic parts and construction logic explicit, supports editing and reuse, and enables deterministic execution and automated verification (Lu et al. (2025); Dai et al. (2025)). Building on these advantages, some approaches generate compositional assets through specialized programmatic interfaces that explicitly represent parts, spatial relation-ships, and structural constraints (Barkley et al. (2026); Li et al. (2026a); Zhou et al. (2026); Lin et al. (2026)).However, these methods are mostly designed for text-conditioned generation. When applied to image-based reconstruction, they often struggle to recover key geometric patterns and inter-part relationships, and their outputs are not necessarily editable B-Reps. In contrast, CADForge is ex-plicitly designed for single-view CAD reconstruction. It combines a tailored part-decomposition strategy with explicit geometric reasoning to recover geometric structure and spatial relationships from the input image and convert them into an editable parametric CAD model. 

## 3 METHOD 

In this section, we present CADForge, which reconstructs a CAD model from an arbitrary image. As illustrated in Fig. 1, CADForge first decomposes the complete object into a set of distinct yet interrelated components and then constructs them part by part (3.2). For each part, our geometry reasoning module carefully analyzes the input image and formulates mathematical programs to solve for the key geometric parameters (3.3). Assisted by a self-constructed toolkit, a code generator then synthesizes the corresponding CadQuery code (3.4). To ensure global consistency without introducing excessive context, we maintain a compact parametric CAD memory that records the evolving CAD state and allows agents to retrieve relevant historical parameters when needed (3.5). 3.1 PROBLEM FORMULATION 

Given a single RGB image I ∈ RH×W ×3 depicting an object, our goal is to reconstruct the object as a part-aware parametric CAD model O = ( B, Q), where B = {B1, . . . , B N } is a list of B-Rep shapes representing the object’s components, and Q = {q1, . . . , q N } contains the corresponding CadQuery programs. Each program qi specifies the geometry and spatial placement of the i-th component, and is executed by a CAD kernel to produce its shape: 

Bi = KernelExecute( qi). (1) Together, the generated components form a coherent reconstruction, while their CadQuery programs preserve parametric editability and construction logic for additional downstream CAD operations. 3.2 COMPONENT DECOMPOSING 

To avoid the ambiguity and complexity of synthesizing an entire object in a single step, we first employ a VLM to decompose the object depicted in the input image into an ordered sequence of CAD-meaningful components: P = [( p1, b 1), . . . , (pN , b N )] , where pi denotes the label of the i-th target component (e.g., seat panel ), and bi is a brief description capturing its semantic and struc-tural characteristics. Each pi represents either an individual physical part or a coherent subassembly. For occluded or unobserved regions, the VLM combines the available visual evidence with learned object priors to infer plausible missing components and their main forms. Rather than relying solely on visual appearance, our decomposition reflects practical CAD work-flows in both granularity and construction order. Elements with shared geometric structures, such as symmetry or repetition, are treated as a single modeling unit, while the sequence P arranges all units according to their dependencies. A key challenge in single-view CAD reconstruction is recovering geometric structure and spatial relationships from incomplete visual information. Existing methods often fail to infer these cues reliably, leading to geometric inconsistencies in the reconstructed model. To address this, we intro-duce a two-stage geometry reasoning module before CAD code generation, consisting of geometry observation and geometry programming to extract and formalize CAD-relevant geometric cues. 3.3.1 GEOMETRY OBSERVATION 

For each component pi, CADForge analyzes the input image I and selects a compact reference set 

Ri from previously constructed components. The selection is guided primarily by spatial relation-ships and shape relevance, favoring parts that are spatially related or structurally analogous to pi.Using Ri as geometric anchors, the geometry observation module then produces a CAD-oriented specification to guide subsequent shape modeling and component assembly: 

gi = Observe( I, p i, b i, Ri). (2) Concretely, gi captures both the target component’s intrinsic structure and its geometric relationships with Ri. At the intra-component level, gi describes its profile, principal orientation, symmetry, and repetition patterns. At the inter-component level, it specifies its pose and scale relative to Ri,together with the attachment, alignment and support relationships, which may include constraints such as parallelism and perpendicularity, as well as relative offsets between components. Unlike implicit visual cues, this explicit and structured representation situates pi relative to the existing geometry and reduces ambiguity, significantly enhancing the consistency of the generated model. 4CADForge: Agentic Single-View CAD Reconstruction with Explicit Geometry Reasoning 3.3.2 GEOMETRY PROGRAMMING 

To bridge the gap between natural-language geometric descriptions and CAD construction, CAD-Forge further introduces Geometry Programming, a module that translates geometric specifications into executable code for computing key modeling parameters. Instead of reasoning about every as-pect of a component, this stage focuses on the essential geometric parameters implied by gi. For each component pi, CADForge first retrieves the structured parameters ΘRi of its reference com-ponents from the parametric CAD memory (3.5), and then uses these parameters along with gi to synthesize a lightweight Python program si, which is executed to obtain the target parameters θi:

si = ProgramLLM( gi, ΘRi ), θi = Exec( si). (3) The resulting θi is returned as a structured dictionary that records each parameter’s name, mean-ing, and numerical value, providing explicit and inspectable geometric guidance for CAD shape modeling. By converting high-level geometric information into precise, construction-ready param-eters, our proposed two-stage Geometry Reasoning greatly enhances geometric accuracy and cross-component consistency while reducing the inference burden for downstream code generation. 3.4 CAD QUERY GENERATION 

3.4.1 CODE SYNTHESIS 

Given the semantic brief bi, geometric specification gi, solved parameters θi, and the reference modeling parameters ΘRi , we employ an LLM-based code generator to synthesize an executable CadQuery program for the current component: 

ci = CodeLLM( pi, b i, g i, θ i, ΘRi ). (4) Here, ci denotes the CadQuery program that constructs pi, which is compiled together with pre-viously generated programs to produce the updated CAD model. Since Geometry Reasoning has already parameterized and resolved the key geometric relationships, the code generator can devote more modeling capacity to generating appropriate CadQuery operations rather than determining spatial configuration, which leads to more stable code synthesis and more faithful CAD geometry. If the generated program fails to execute or produces CAD output that does not pass the validity checks, a code repairer revises the CadQuery code based on the associated error message. After successful execution, CADForge renders the updated model from two complementary views, with new components highlighted in different colors, and concatenates them with the input image for review. The Review Agent then checks whether the generated component matches the intended shape, scale, and position. If a failure is detected, it attributes the error to either geometry reasoning or code synthesis and routes the revision back to the corresponding stage with targeted feedback. 3.4.2 FAILURE -G UIDED TOOLKIT SELF -C ONSTRUCTION 

Although CadQuery provides a powerful procedural framework for CAD modeling, synthesizing reliable CadQuery code remains challenging due to its stateful, selector-driven modeling paradigm and strict geometric validity requirements. Even syntactically correct programs may still fail or produce invalid geometry. To further improve robustness and prevent recurring implementation errors, we propose a failure-guided mechanism for automatically constructing a reusable toolkit. Specifically, we construct a separate set from the Fusion 360 dataset (Willis et al. (2021)) and run our pipeline on the corresponding rendered images, recording complete generation traces that include intermediate observations, geometry programs, synthesized code, execution results, and review feed-back. From these traces, we mine two complementary types of failure cases. The first consists of runs with repeated code execution failures, which reveal CadQuery operations that LLMs struggle to implement reliably. The second includes geometrically inaccurate generations, identified by ranking all cases based on the Chamfer Distance and selecting the worst 30% as low-quality cases. An LLM then analyzes these cases together with their generation traces to summarize recurring failure pat-terns and identify the underlying CAD operations responsible for them. Based on these summaries, we prompt the LLM to encapsulate frequently used yet error-prone modeling operations into robust, reusable helper functions, such as those for solid composition and hole construction. Only helpers that have been verified as effective are incorporated into the toolkit. As new failure traces are col-lected, the same process can be repeated to expand the toolkit incrementally. Since CADForge constructs an object component by component, each generation step may depend on the dimensions, spatial placement, and attachment interfaces of previously generated compo-nents. Passing the full construction history to the LLM would introduce substantial redundancy and token overhead. To avoid this, CADForge maintains a lightweight parametric CAD memory, implemented as a nested dictionary in which each accepted component is associated with its im-portant modeling parameters. Specifically, when generating the CadQuery program for pi, CAD-Forge further abstracts the underlying modeling operations into a structured parameter dictionary

Evaluation Set . To comprehensively assess our method, we construct an evaluation set of 200 samples. Among them, 170 samples are collected from Objaverse (Deitke et al. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

We present CADForge, a training-free agentic framework for single-view CAD reconstruction. CADForge progressively reconstructs CAD models by decomposing objects into components, ex-plicitly reasoning about their geometry, and iteratively refining the generated programs, while im-proving robustness and efficiency through a failure-guided toolkit and compact parametric memory. Experiments show that CADForge achieves higher reconstruction fidelity with lower token con-sumption than existing baselines. CAD Program Validity Rate . For CAD generation, reliably producing valid B-reps is particularly important. Final generation success alone, however, does not fully characterize execution stability for agentic methods, since failed programs can often be iteratively repaired or regenerated until a valid result is eventually obtained. Two methods may therefore achieve the same final success rate while requiring substantially different numbers of failed executions and repair attempts. To capture this behavior, we additionally record all code execution attempts during inference and report the CAD Program Validity Rate (CPVR) as a complementary measure of execution stability. We therefore follow each method’s na-tive execution procedure rather than artificially imposing a unified repair protocol. To expose the toolkit to the LLM, we include a compact API manifest in the prompts for initial code generation, component synthesis, and code repair. The Code Agent is encouraged to invoke a helper whenever it matches the required modeling operation, while retaining native CadQuery primitives for geometries not covered by the toolkit. D.1 TEXTURE GENERATION AND ARTICULATION MODELING 

Since CADForge represents an object as part-wise B-rep geometry with executable modeling code, its output can be readily augmented with additional appearance and articulation attributes.
