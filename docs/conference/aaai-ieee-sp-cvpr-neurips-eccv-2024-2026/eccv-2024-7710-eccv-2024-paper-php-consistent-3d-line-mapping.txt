Abstract. We address the problem of reconstructing 3D line segments along with line tracks from multiple views with known camera poses. The basic pipeline is first generating 3D line segment proposals for each 2D line segment, then selecting the best proposals, merging them to pro-duce 3D line segments and line tracks, and finally performing non-linear optimization. Our key contributions are focused on exploring and alle-viating the inconsistency problems in classical approaches. In the best proposal selection, we analyze the inherent inconsistency problem of sup-port relationships from 2D to 3D determined during proposal evaluation using multiple views and propose an iterative algorithm to handle it. In line track building, we impose 2D collinearity constraints to enhance the consistency of the elements in each line track. In optimization, we intro-duce coplanarity constraints and jointly optimize points, lines, planes, and vanishing points, enhancing the consistency of the structure of the line map. Experimental results demonstrate that our emphasis on con-sistency enables our line maps to achieve state-of-the-art completeness and accuracy, while also generating longer and more robust line tracks. Code is available at https://github.com/3dv-casia/clmap .

Keywords: 3D Line Mapping · Consistent Reconstruction · 3D Line Optimization 

## 1 Introduction 

Compared to 3D points, 3D line segments have lower redundancy and are better suited for representing weak textures and slender structures. They are also com-monly found in man-made environments. The produced 3D line segments map has been applied to many other tasks, including visual localization [24, 30, 47], surface reconstruction [18, 29, 46], pose refinement [24] and mesh refinement [5]. According to the difference of line matching manner, the classical 3D line mapping approaches can be classified into one-to-one matching, and without one-to-one matching methods. For one-to-one matching methods, such as [2, 3, 7,16,19,31,44,54], only one-to-one 2D line segment correspondences are permit-ted. These methods predominantly concentrate on line matching and subsequent

Typically, these methods operate by identifying the top K line matches in a neighboring image for each 2D line segment. Subsequently, they use each match to triangulate one or more 3D line segment proposals (referred to as pro-posals in this paper) corresponding to the reference 2D line segment. The best proposal is then selected, and clustering is performed to generate 3D line segments and line tracks. Finally, non-linear optimization is employed for lines. Compared to one-to-one matching, the top K matching manner reduces the demand for 2D line matching accuracy and more possible 3D lines can be pro-duced. Hence, our method falls into the second category. However, more outlier matches present a significant challenge for reconstruction. In classical meth-ods of line reconstruction without one-to-one matching, e.g ., L3D++ [11] and LIMAP [24], we find three inconsistency issues. 1) In the best proposal selection module, classical methods independently select the best proposal for each 2D line segment. This undermines the consistency of the support relationships. Initially, we assume a matched 2D line segment supports a proposal based on a repro-jection test. However, after selecting its own best proposal, this best proposal and the supported proposal may not be collinear, leading to the inconsistency of the support relationship. 2) In the line track building module, classical methods often neglect 2D collinearity constraints, potentially resulting in the assignment of two non-collinear 2D line segments within the same image to the same line track. Such an assignment would be inconsistent with the definition of a line track. Note that a line track is a set of 2D line segments on several images observing certain parts of a 3D line segment entity. 3) In the optimization mod-ule, classical methods miss the coplanarity constraints among lines, leading to inconsistencies between the structure represented by the line map and the real-ity. While the method in SLAM [20] jointly optimizes points, lines, and planes, the current line-to-plane distance is affected by the position of the coordinates system, causing discrepancies in distances for identical line-plane configurations across different locations in 3D. Furthermore, classical methods often manually set weights for energy terms with different units, which seems highly ad hoc. In this paper, we establish a systematic approach for consistent 3D line map-ping, presenting the following primary contributions: 

– We first improve the proposal generation module by leveraging unshared 3D points and then propose an iterative algorithm to update the best proposals by leveraging the previous best proposals of the matched 2D line segments for scoring, thereby enhancing the consistency of the support relationships. 

– We propose a strategy for generating line tracks based on the 2D collinearity constraint of the track elements. This prevents two non-collinear 2D line segments within the same image from being assigned to the same line track, thereby enhancing the consistency of the elements in each line track.

## 2 Related Work 

Line Reconstruction with One-to-one Matching. Based on the 2D line segment detectors [14, 32, 33, 42, 49] and descriptors [41, 52, 53], various meth-ods of line reconstruction with one-to-one matching are proposed. Hartley and Zisserman [7] summarized Plücker matrices and Plücker coordinates of line and multi-view line triangulation methods. Bartoli and Sturm [3] proposed the or-thonormal representation of line. Josephson and Kahl [16] proposed a statisti-cally optimal multi-view line triangulation method. Wei et al . [44] proposed a line-matching method based on planes and points and selected the representa-tive 3D line segments to produce final 3D line segments. Zhang and Koch [54] proposed the Cayley representation of line and built a line-based Structure-from-Motion (SfM) system. There are also other efforts dedicated to line-based SfM [31, 34, 36, 40, 45]. Recently, the line features have also been integrated with points [6,8,43,48,50], planes [20,39,51,55] or vanishing points [13,21] in SLAM to improve the localization performance. However, these methods, relying on pre-cise one-to-one matching, face serious challenges with too many outlier matches. 

Line Reconstruction without One-to-one Matching. To handle the prob-lem of limited line description, some methods of line reconstruction do not strictly require one-to-one matching. Jain et al . [15] proposed a line reconstruc-tion method based on a sweeping approach [4] and connectivity constraints, which eliminates the need for explicit matches. However, this method is time-consuming and cannot accurately reconstruct line segments outside the sweeping range. Subsequently, Hofer et al . [12] mitigated these drawbacks by employing weak epipolar constraints to identify multiple candidate matches for generating 3D line segment proposals. This approach has undergone further development in their subsequent works [9,10], culminating in the Line3D++ [11] package (re-ferred to as L3D++ in this paper). Liu et al . ## 3 Consistent 3D Line Mapping 

Starting with the posed images, our process begins by detecting 2D line seg-ments and retrieving the top K line matches from each of the nv neighboring images. Then, we create 3D line segment proposals and determine the best pro-posal for each 2D line segment. These best proposals are merged to build line tracks. Lastly, we perform joint optimization involving points, lines, planes, and vanishing points. An overview of the pipeline is depicted in Fig. 2.

For each 2D line segment, we utilize all its matches to generate its 3D line segment proposals. In both the Multiple Points and Line+Point methods, a crucial step involves establishing the association between 2D lines and 3D points. or lm, but not both, as unshared 3D points. Unlike LIMAP [24], our method, illustrated in Fig. 3, goes beyond utilizing shared 3D points. We also incorpo-rate unshared 3D points to establish associations, ensuring that these unshared 3D points pass the reprojection error test. This contributes significantly to the overall completeness of the line map.

3.3 Line Track Building 

We cluster 2D line segments to build line tracks. A graph is first constructed where nodes denote 2D line segments with valid best proposals, and edges are initialized as the top K line matches and weighted by the similarity score of the best proposals of two nodes. Edges with a zero weight will be removed. We then create an edge list sorted in descending order of weights, iterating through it from the beginning to merge two nodes of each edge using the Union-Find algorithm [38]. To enhance the consistency of the line track, we try to avoid two non-collinear 2D line segments within the same image from being assigned to the same line track. To this end, when merging two groups G1 and G2, we avoid merging them if there exist two 2D line segments l1 ∈ G 1 and l2 ∈ G 2

simultaneously satisfying the following conditions: 

– l1 and l2 are on the same image, 

– da(l1, l 2) > t a ∨ dp(l1, l 2) > t p ∨ do(l1, l 2) > t o,where da(·, ·) computes angle of two 2D line segments, dp(·, ·) computes maxi-mum perpendicular distance from all 2D endpoints to the 2D infinite lines formed by two 2D line segments, do(·, ·) computes the ratio of bi-overlap by mutually projecting two line segments onto each other to compute the maximum ratio of intersection. We set the thresholds ta = 2 degrees, tp = 2 pixels, to = 0 in this paper. Finally, the 2D line segments within the same Union-Find group form a line track. For each line track, we apply the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) method for all directions of best proposals and use the principal eigen-vector and mean 3D endpoints of best proposals to fit a 3D infinite line. All 3D endpoints are projected to this 3D infinite line to get new robust 3D endpoints.

4.2 Consistency Enhancing and More Insights in Best Proposal Selection. 

To evaluate the consistency between the support relationships and the best pro-posals of the supporting 2D line segments, we introduce a new metric: 

– Consistency percentage at τa/τd (CP τ a/τ d): the percentage of supporting 2D line segments for a supported proposal, where the angle between the supported proposal and the best proposal of the supporting 2D line segment is within τa degrees, and the maximum perpendicular distance from each endpoint of one line to the other line is within τd millimeters. This metric uses collinearity to evaluate consistency. Each supported proposal can compute a consistency percentage at τa/τd. A smaller consistency percentage indicates more severe inconsistency issues. In our algorithm, each iteration generates new best proposals along with the corresponding new support relationships. To demonstrate that our iterative algorithm gradually mitigates inconsistency and improves performance, we dis-played some results evolving with iterations in Fig. 7. ## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose a consistent 3D line mapping system that enhances the consistency of support relationships from 2D to 3D, the consistency of the elements in each line track, and the consistency of the structure of the line map. Experimental evaluations showcase the superior performance of our system when compared to state-of-the-art alternatives. However, due to the use of the iterative algorithm and joint optimization with planes, our method is a bit slower than LIMAP [24]. The average running time of our method on each Hypersim scene (100 images per) [35] is 60 seconds on a machine with a 3.4GHz processor, compared to 51 seconds for LIMAP [24]. Since our contributions focus on 3D line mapping given line correspondences and VPs, the time for feature detection and matching is not included. In the future, we will dedicate ourselves to building a more consistent and efficient 3D line mapping system. Consistent 3D Line Mapping 15
