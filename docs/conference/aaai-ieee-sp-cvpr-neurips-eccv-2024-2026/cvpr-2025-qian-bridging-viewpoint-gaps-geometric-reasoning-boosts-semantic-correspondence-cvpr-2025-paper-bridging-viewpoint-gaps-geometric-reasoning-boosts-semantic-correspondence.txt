## Abstract 

Finding semantic correspondences between images is a challenging problem in computer vision, particularly un-der significant viewpoint changes. Previous methods rely on semantic features from pre-trained 2D models like Sta-ble Diffusion and DINOv2, which often struggle to extract viewpoint-invariant features. To overcome this, we propose a novel approach that integrates geometric and semantic reasoning. Unlike prior methods relying on heuristic ge-ometric enhancements, our framework fine-tunes DUSt3R on synthetic cross-instance data to reconstruct distinct ob-jects in an aligned 3D space. By learning to deform these objects into similar shapes using semantic supervision, we enable efficient KNN-based geometric matching, followed by sparse semantic matching within local KNN candidates. While trained on synthetic data, our method generalizes ef-fectively to real-world images, achieving up to 7.4-point im-provements in zero-shot settings on the rigid-body subset of Spair-71K and up to 19.6-point gains under extreme view-point variations. Additionally, it accelerates runtime by up to 40 times, demonstrating both its robustness to view-point changes and its efficiency for practical applications. Code is available at https://github.com/Qiyang-Q/Bridge-Viewpoint-Gap .

## 1. Introduction 

Finding semantic correspondences aims at the identifica-tion of semantically similar points across different images. Establishing these correspondences enables various appli-cations, such as content editing, visual retrieval, and style transfer, etc. The challenge of this task arises from both the variations in appearance and the changes in viewpoint. Recent vision representation models [5, 16, 42, 50] have made significant strides in learning invariant representations that are robust to changes in appearance. Yet, these mod-els are primarily trained on 2D images and lack an under-

standing of 3D geometry. This limitation makes it difficult for these models to find accurate semantic correspondences when there are substantial viewpoint changes. To tackle this issue, previous work has attempted to in-corporate geometric awareness into semantic features to make representations more robust to viewpoint variations and improve 3D understanding. For example, Telling Left from Right [61] tries to incorporate perspective informa-tion by finetuning on some 2D direction basis like left and right, but this is not sufficient to handle drastic 3D per-spective changes. Spherical map [34] back-projects the 2D features into a spherical space to guide the semantic cor-respondences. However, it requires supervised finetuning on specific target domains. To achieve generalizability, Xu 

et al . [58] try to directly inject the 3D awareness into Sta-ble Diffusion by training a ControlNet on large-scale posed videos. However, it can only accommodate minor view-point alterations. Moreover, finding the best correspon-dences on dense feature maps is computationally intensive, thereby impeding their practical application. To address these challenges, we are inspired by a fun-damental principle: semantic correspondences exhibit ge-ometric consistency in 3D space, regardless of viewpoint variations in 2D images. Therefore, we propose a new approach: instead of injecting geometric awareness when learning the semantic features, we bridge the viewpoint gap by first establishing coarse correspondences purely through geometric reasoning then refining the correspondences us-ing semantic alignment. Specifically, we make a novel use of the advanced model, DUSt3R [54], a robust model orig-inally designed for 3D reconstruction from two views. Unlike conventional reconstruction tasks that rely on two views of the same object, we fine-tune DUSt3R with cross-instance data to reconstruct two distinct 3D objects within a unified 3D space. To accomplish this, we leverage the ex-tensive synthetic data from ShapeNet [6], collecting cross-instance image pairs with significant viewpoint variations. After finetuning on the synthetic data, DUSt3R is capable of reconstructing two instances simultaneously with aligned poses. More importantly, our method in a zero-shot manner is robust to significant changes in viewpoint even outperforming in-domain finetuned method, which high-lights its vast potential for practical use. 

## 2. Related Work 

Image Correspondence. Establishing correspondences between images is a fundamental task in computer vision, with applications in Structure-from-Motion/3D reconstruc-tion [1, 39, 45, 46], object tracking [12, 59], image recog-nition [4, 40, 49] and segmentation [15, 25, 28, 43]. Visual correspondence and semantic correspondence, while differ-ing in whether they focus on the same instance or differ-ent instances within the same category, share the common goal of learning features invariant to illumination, occlu-sion, articulation, viewpoint, and even semantic changes. Traditional methods rely on hand-crafted features such as SIFT [30] and SURF [3] to find correspondences. With the advent of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been employed to learn feature representa-tions for correspondence tasks. Supervised methods have shown promising results by learning representations from ground-truth correspondence annotations [9, 18, 22, 23]. To address the scarcity of labeled data, weakly supervised [52] and self-supervised methods [20, 56] have been developed, 11580 allowing models to learn correspondences without explicit annotations. Recently, transformers have been introduced to correspondence problems. Methods using transformers leverage attention mechanisms to learn robust representa-tions that capture both spatial relationships and visual ap-pearance [22, 27, 44, 53]. 

Pre-trained Representations from Generative and Foun-dation Models. Recent works have utilized representa-tions learned from generative models, such as Genera-tive Adversarial Networks (GANs) [13], diffusion mod-els [17, 21, 47, 48], and visual foundation models such as DINO [5, 38] and OpenCLIP [19], to improve semantic cor-respondence. These pretrained representations exhibit an emergent ability to capture rich semantic information orig-inating from tasks like image generation and generalize ef-fectively across various datasets and tasks. Such methods have changed the paradigm of finding semantic correspon-dences by providing features that can be used directly for matching [50, 60]. Subsequent methods have investigated how to use these features as frozen backbones and add ad-ditional modules for better correspondence [31, 60, 61]. However, these representations are not without limitations. Recent works [60, 61] point out that these representations struggle when handling large viewpoint changes, indicating that they lack 3D awareness. 

Learning 3D-Aware Representations. To address the limitations of 2D representations under significant view-point changes, some previous work has investigated how to incorporate 3D information into representations learned from 2D image planes. Techniques like Neural Radi-ance Fields (NeRF) [35] learn implicit representations through volumetric rendering, and NeRF-based object de-tection [57] demonstrates that such representations perform well on 3D detection tasks. Other methods use camera pose or object pose information to learn invariant features with respect to changes in camera view [2, 7, 56, 58]. In ad-dition, some approaches extend this idea to semantic cor-respondence. For example, [61] introduces a pose-variant augmentation in test time through flipping to align images, while [34] injects geometric priors into learned representa-tions using viewpoint information. 

Explicit 3D Reasoning. Some previous work [26, 51] at-tempted to directly map image pixels into a 3D space by predicting a canonical 3D coordinate for each pixel. How-ever, these methods force the network to learn a fixed co-ordinate space. Recently DUSt3R [54] proposes to jointly map two images in a shared 3D coordinate space, al-lowing robust cross-view alignment. Based on DUSt3R, MASt3R [24] refines this idea for image correspondence, highlighting the ability of such joint 3D representations to produce accurate matches across views. 

3D Deformation for dense correspondence. While map-ping images from the same category into a shared 3D co-ordinate space can effectively capture global spatial rela-tionships, such approaches struggle with intra-category ge-ometric variations. Alongside direct 3D mapping, some approaches use point cloud deformation to establish dense correspondences between category-level objects. For in-stance, [55] learns per-vertex offsets to warp a source model, [8] maps point clouds to a canonical primitive and deforms them for correspondence, and [10] represents 3D shapes as deformed templates implicit field using dedicated deformation and correction fields. These works show that existing 3D deformation methods can capture geometric variations, enabling robust semantic correspondence. Building upon these insights, we propose a method that combines the advantages of pre-trained representations from visual foundation models with explicit 3D geomet-ric reasoning. By integrating semantic features extracted from models like DINO with joint 3D coordinate map-ping and deformation-based refinement, our approach effec-tively handles semantic correspondence under large view-point changes.

With the deformation head, two images in different in-stances are now not only aligned in orientation but also matched in shape and semantics in the unified coordinate space. During inference time, we can establish correspon-dences by geometric matching using 3D nearest neighbor. In addition to geometric matching, we further propose a test-time semantic matching approach, formulating a hybrid matching strategy for finer-level semantic correspondence. For coarse geometric matching, we use the predicted point maps of the source and target images,  X1 and  X2,to construct k-d trees for efficient nearest neighbor searches in 3D space. Given a set of keypoints  P in the source im-age, we identify the  K nearest neighbors in 3D space from the target image’s pointmap  X2. These neighbors serve as candidate matching points in the target image. For fine-grained semantic matching, we extract dense se-mantic feature maps  f 1 and  f 2 for both images using a pre-trained feature extractor. For each keypoint in  P , we calculate the cosine similarity between its semantic feature in  f 1 and the semantic features in  f 2 corresponding to the 

K nearest neighbors identified during geometric matching. The final correspondence is determined by points with high-est similarity from the candidates. 

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Implementation details 

Training Dataset. We generate a comprehensive and pho-torealistic dataset using ShapeNetCore[6] 3D models that provide consistently categorized pose-aligned 3D objects. For each object within the same category, we first normal-ize its size to ensure uniformity across all instances by scal-ing it using the factor  s = 1max( x,y,z ) , where  x, y, z  are the lengths of the object’s bounding box in 3 dimensions. We then rendered both RGB and depth images for each nor-malized object using 100 predefined camera rotations with uniform increments in azimuth and elevation angles, ensur-ing comprehensive coverage of views and consistency of the same camera pose in different objects across the entire cate-gory. To mitigate the simulation-to-reality gap and enhance the realism of our dataset, we utilize Blender’s ray tracing engine with High Dynamic Range Imaging (HDRI) envi-ronments for real-world illumination conditions. As a re-sult, we generate an extensive dataset, covering 55 common object categories, comprising 5,130,000 RGB and depth im-ages at a resolution of  512 × 512  pixels, using 51,300 mod-els from ShapeNetCore, resulting in a total dataset size of approximately 1.3 terabytes. 

Inference in real world image. We first generate a mask of the object in the image following [61] using the SAM2 model [41]. In the geometric matching stage, we predict 3D pointmaps for both the source and target images at a reso-lution of  224 × 224  pixels. We then construct two KD trees [32] for efficient K nearest neighbor search. For each key-point in the source image, we search for its corresponding 

K nearest neighbors on the target point map using the KD trees. In the semantic matching stage, we employ semantic feature maps of size  840 × 840  pixels. We upsample the 

K pixels identified during the geometric matching from the 

224 × 224  resolution to the  840 × 840  resolution to obtain maximum cosine similarity between the semantic feature of the source keypoint and the features within the localized re-gion of the target image for final correspondence. 

Evaluation Datasets. We conduct experiments on widely used benchmarks SPair-71k [36], a challenging semantic correspondence dataset that contains a diverse set of 53,340 training pairs, 5,384 validation pairs, and 12,234 testing pairs spanning 18 object categories. This dataset presents significant variations in the viewpoint, scale, and object in-stances. We focus our evaluation on the rigid body cate-gories , specifically, 11 categories in SPair-71k. 

Evaluation Metric. We adopt the widely used percentage of correct keypoints (PCK) metric. The PCK measures the proportion of predicted keypoints that are correctly matched to the ground truth within a specified threshold. A predicted keypoint is considered correct if it lies within a threshold of  α × max( h, w ) pixels from the ground truth keypoint, where  α ∈ [0 , 1]  (e.g.  α = 0 .10 ), and  h and 

w represent height and width, respectively. Specifically, for the SPair-71k dataset,  h and  w are the dimensions of the 

bounding box of an instance ( αbbox ) and we report all re-sults using PCK per point for consistency in metric. 

Model Variants and Baselines. we evaluate our baseline model and its variants, comparing them with state-of-the-art methods. Our baseline, Ours (Geo Only) only uses geometric matching for 1 nearest neighbor search. This baseline is enhanced with two variants on top of geomet-ric matching for K = 300 nearest neighbors: Ours (Geo + Semantic (DINOv2 ViT-S)) uses semantic matching from descriptors extracted by DINOv2 ViT-S and Ours (Geo + Semantic (DINOv2 + SD) uses semantic matching from descriptors extracted from DINOv2 ViT-B and Stable Dif-fusion (SD); these two descriptors are fused following [60]. We compare our methods with existing approaches: [50], [60], which only uses unsupervised semantic repre-sentation learned from the 2D image plane and [61] and [34], which incorporate geometric or spatial information into semantic representations. The methods we compare can therefore be grouped into 2 categories: • Group (a) : Methods that rely on 2D representation • Group (b) : Methods that inject geometric information. [34, 61]. 4.4. Limitations 

Synthetic Data Quality and Ambiguity Despite promis-ing results, our method has certain limitations. First, per-formance is heavily dependent on the quality of the 3D model. For instance, our lower performance on buses arises from overly simplistic ShapeNet meshes, leading to greater simulation-to-real gaps and ambiguity, particularly because the front and back appear similar. This ambiguity causes the network, under the l2 loss, to regress positions toward the midpoint of ambiguous ground-truth points. 

Limitation in deformable categories Our current pipeline is limited to rigid-body objects for several reasons. First, most publicly available animal datasets provide only in-the-wild RGB images without accompanying depth maps or consistent camera poses in a unified coordinate system. Also, we assume orientation-aligned objects in 3D datasets. For nonrigid classes such as animals, where defor-mation introduces an effectively infinite number of degrees of freedom, defining a canonical ’orientation’ is inherently difficult. One direction to address the non-rigid issue involves leveraging parametric models like SMAL[62] or SMPL[29]. These models can align real-world images to a common template, potentially offering a structured way to extend our method beyond rigid categories. 

## 5. Conclusion 

Semantic correspondence faces two main challenges: ap-pearance variations and viewpoint changes. We address this challenge by extending DUSt3R for cross-instance view-point alignment. To address the lack of annotations and adapt DUSt3R for shape differences, we propose an effi-cient synthetic data annotation strategy using coordinate-aligned 3D models yet under various viewpoints. Our method introduces a novel hybrid pipeline that combines geometric alignment and feature-based local refinement. Despite training on synthetic data, it achieves superior zero-shot generalization to real-world scenarios, delivering sig-nificant performance improvements under drastic viewpoint changes while achieving high efficiency. 11586 References
